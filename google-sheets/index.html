Google Sheets keyboard shortcuts

Google Sheets borrows many of its shortcuts from Excel, so the two feel alike. Because Sheets runs in a browser, a few combinations are different, and some need a setting switched on. The first column is for Windows and Chromebooks, the second for the Mac.

The ten to learn first

  1. Ctrl + / shows every shortcut. The same panel has a switch for "compatible spreadsheet shortcuts", which makes more of Excel's keys work.
  2. Ctrl + Arrow key jumps to the edge of the data.
  3. Ctrl + Shift + Arrow key selects to the edge of the data.
  4. Ctrl + Space selects the column and Shift + Space the row.
  5. F2 or Enter edits the cell.
  6. F4 cycles the dollar signs on a reference while editing.
  7. Ctrl + Enter fills the selected range with what you typed.
  8. Ctrl + Shift + V pastes values only.
  9. Ctrl + ; inserts today's date.
  10. Ctrl + Shift + Enter wraps a formula in ARRAYFORMULA.

Switch on the Excel-style shortcuts

Open the shortcut panel with Ctrl + / and turn on Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts. More of the combinations used in other spreadsheet programs then work in Sheets. The same panel is a searchable list of every shortcut for your own system, and it is the final word when this page and your keyboard disagree.

The browser gets some keys first

Sheets shares the keyboard with the browser. Ctrl + T opens a browser tab, not a table. Ctrl + W closes the whole file's tab. A few of the spreadsheet shortcuts people know from Excel, such as Ctrl + Page Down for the next sheet, are taken by the browser for its own tabs, which is why Sheets uses Alt + Down arrow.

Paste values only

Ctrl + Shift + V pastes the result of a formula without the formula, and text without its formatting. It is the way to freeze a calculated column, or to bring in data from a website without its colours and fonts.

Fill a whole range at once

Select a range, type a value or a formula, and press Ctrl + Enter. Every selected cell gets it, with references adjusted as if you had copied the formula down. To fill a formula down a long column, select from the formula cell to the bottom with Ctrl + Shift + Down arrow, then press Ctrl + D.

Nothing to save

As in Docs, every change is saved as it happens, and File, Version history keeps earlier states. For formulas and functions, see our Google Sheets site.
