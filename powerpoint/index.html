PowerPoint keyboard shortcuts

PowerPoint shortcuts fall into two sets: the ones for building slides, and the ones for the moment you stand up and present. The second set matters most, because fumbling with a mouse in front of an audience is what people remember. The first column is for Windows and the second for the Mac.

The eight to learn first

  1. F5 starts the show from the first slide.
  2. Shift + F5 starts from the slide you are on, which is what you want when rehearsing.
  3. B blanks the screen to black during a show, and brings it back. Use it when you want the room to look at you.
  4. A number, then Enter jumps straight to that slide during a show.
  5. Esc ends the show.
  6. Ctrl + M adds a new slide.
  7. Ctrl + D duplicates the selected slide or object.
  8. Ctrl + G groups the selected objects.

Presenting without touching the mouse

Know your slide numbers. Print the outline with numbers, or keep them on your notes. When a question takes you back to an earlier slide, type its number and press Enter. It looks far more assured than clicking back through twenty slides.

Black out the screen on purpose. People cannot listen to you and read a slide at once. Press B when you want their attention, and B again to bring the slide back. W does the same with a white screen, which is better in a dark room where you need some light.

Use Presenter View with a projector. Alt + F5 shows you the current slide, the next one, your notes and a clock, while the audience sees only the slide.

Rehearse with Shift + F5. It starts from wherever you are, so you can practise one section without sitting through the opening each time.

Draw, then erase. Ctrl + P turns the pointer into a pen for circling a figure. E clears the marks. When the show ends, PowerPoint asks whether to keep them.

Building faster

Duplicate, do not rebuild. Ctrl + D on a finished slide gives a copy with the same layout. Change the text and you have a consistent next slide. On an object, Ctrl + D repeated places copies at the same spacing each time, which lines up a row of boxes neatly.

Group things that belong together. Select the parts of a diagram and press Ctrl + G. They then move and resize as one.

Hold Shift for straight lines and true shapes. A line drawn with Shift held is exactly horizontal or vertical. A rectangle becomes a square, an oval a circle.

Select hidden objects with Tab. When shapes overlap and you cannot click the one underneath, press Tab to step through every object on the slide.
